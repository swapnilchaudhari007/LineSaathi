# Idea Submission: Form Answers

**Challenge:** Voice-Powered Enterprise Knowledge & Decision Agent

## What is your idea about?
LineSaathi is a real-time, voice-to-voice AI agent for electricity distribution (DISCOM) linemen, who work on poles and transformers with gloves on and both hands busy. Built on the Gemini Live API (native audio), it lets a lineman just talk in Marathi, Hindi or Hinglish, through a Bluetooth helmet headset or a plain phone call from any feature phone. LineSaathi answers from DISCOM knowledge (SOPs, CEA safety rules, OEM manuals, past tickets, even if written in English) and pulls live asset history. Then it acts: it raises the shutdown permit-to-work (PTW), logs the fault, orders material from the store and closes the consumer complaint. Its USP is the Voice Safety Gate, a deterministic PTW state machine the LLM cannot skip. No "line clear" or re-charge step runs until the lineman reads it back aloud and the operator confirms. It handles interruptions and background noise, cites the SOP clause it used, and gives supervisors a live board with a full audit trail.

## What problem are you trying to solve?
Linemen keep India's power on, but they work blind and slow. To restore one fault, a lineman on a pole makes 3-4 phone calls (substation operator, JE, store), fills paper permit books, and cannot look up SOPs or asset history because DISCOM apps are English-only, typing-based and need a smartphone and data. Knowledge sits in English PDFs and old tickets, while the workforce thinks in Marathi or Hindi. The result is slow restoration, no digital audit trail, and unsafe "line clear / re-charge" miscommunication. Electricity-related accidents killed 18,000+ people in India in 2023 (NCRB ADSI data via Prayas), and 61% of electrical accidents in 2025 happened in distribution networks. We solve this with a hands-free voice agent that understands code-switched speech in noisy field conditions, grounds every answer in DISCOM documents, and executes the follow-up action (PTW, fault ticket, material indent, complaint closure) safely, with a spoken read-back before anything runs.

## Technology stack
AI/Agent: Gemini Live API (native audio-to-audio) on Vertex AI with function calling and barge-in; Agent Development Kit (ADK); Vertex AI Agent Engine; Gemini Enterprise (the same agent for office staff); Vertex AI Search / RAG Engine with Gemini embeddings for cross-lingual retrieval over SOPs, CEA regulations, OEM manuals and past tickets.
Backend/Data: Python (FastAPI + WebSockets) on Cloud Run as the voice gateway and tool router; Firestore for the Safety Gate PTW state machine and the mock DISCOM OMS; BigQuery for the asset registry, fault history and store inventory; Cloud Storage; Pub/Sub; Secret Manager; Cloud Logging audit trail; region asia-south1.
Voice/Telephony: Exotel / Twilio Media Streams (SIP to WebSocket bridge) so any feature phone works; 16 kHz PCM streaming; DTMF fallback in extreme noise; Bluetooth headset via the Android companion app.
Frontend/Ops: Flutter companion app, React supervisor Live Board with latency meter, Looker Studio, Cloud Build CI/CD.

## Impact
Safety: every shutdown permit gets a spoken read-back, operator confirmation and a timestamped digital record, which directly targets the miscommunication behind line accidents. Productivity (pilot targets): about 30 min saved per lineman per day on phone-tag and paperwork, and 15-20 min faster restoration per fault, so fewer outage minutes for consumers. Inclusion: a feature-phone-first design in the lineman's own language means no smartphone, data or English needed. Commercial viability: about Rs 5.4 per 2.5-min call (Gemini Live audio + telephony + cloud), about Rs 1,120 per lineman per month run cost, sold at Rs 1,999 per lineman per month SaaS (~44% gross margin). A 50-lineman pilot costs about Rs 56k per month. Scale: 50+ Indian distribution utilities, and the same agent pattern carries over to telecom tower, gas pipeline and water utility field teams. Metrics tracked live: time to first audio, task completion rate, confirmations, and fault-to-restore time.
